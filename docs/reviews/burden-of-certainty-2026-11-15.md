# Scholarly source audit: *The Burden of Certainty*

Date: 15 November 2026
Scope: Single article, `the-burden-of-certainty.html`, and its cited primary textual sources.
Status: Text-critical and citation audit completed for the passages used. **Not a comprehensive review of the entire website or manuscripts.**

## Method
Compare claims against the linked original papers, translated primary works, Qur'anic Arabic and Pickthall's credited translations, and the full Arabic text of the cited classical commentary. Distinguish **textual claims**, **authorial inference**, and **hypothetical illustration**. Preserve the article's three Arabic Qur'an passages and its attributed English translations unchanged.

## Corrections made to the reviewed article
1. Explicitly limit the 49:6 textual imperative to the case described in the verse. Applying it to testimony in an ordinary school allegation is framed as an ethical analogy, **not** calling any pupil a *fāsiq*.
2. Mark Gettier's case as a philosophical analogy, not a direct account of school discipline.
3. Support the hearing/seeing distinction directly with al-Ṭabarī's Arabic commentary on 17:36.
4. Support and qualify the privacy/suspicion discussion with direct Arabic commentary on 49:12.
5. Restore in-text citation [3], which was previously in the references list but not linked from the body.
6. Retain all three Arabic Qur'an passages, the Pickthall-attributed English translations, and the article's existing conclusion.

## Limitations and outstanding work
- This is a **source-to-claim audit**, not an independent textual-critical collation of the Qur'an or all manuscript witnesses to al-Ghazālī and al-Ṭabarī.
- The teacher and pupils are **a hypothetical example**, not an empirical school investigation or factual allegation.
- The article makes normative educational inferences. Primary sources support the referenced quotations and interpretations but do not independently prove every ethical recommendation.
- Wider peer review of the philosophical argument, bibliographic edition comparisons, and a review of all other site articles remain separate projects.
- Any changes affecting school records or the ABMT project are explicitly outside scope.
